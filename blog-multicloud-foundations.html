A practical foundation for governed multi-cloud

Standardise the control points that manage risk while allowing each cloud to contribute its strongest native capabilities.

7 min readCloud architecture guide

Multi-cloud should be an intentional operating model, not a collection of unrelated accounts and subscriptions. A workable foundation defines common controls for identity, connectivity, security, policy, observability and delivery while avoiding an artificial lowest-common-denominator platform.

Begin with clear principles

  • Use a cloud because it supports a defined business or technology outcome.
  • Centralise policy and evidence, not every engineering decision.
  • Prefer portable operational practices over forced workload portability.
  • Keep account, subscription and project structures aligned to ownership and blast radius.

Create one identity and access model

Federate workforce identities from the enterprise identity provider, map access to roles and groups, enforce strong authentication and remove standing privilege where possible. Workload identities should be separate from human identities and use short-lived credentials.

Design connectivity as a product

Define approved ingress, egress, east-west and hybrid patterns. Standardise DNS, IP management, private connectivity and inspection points. Teams should be able to consume connectivity through automation rather than individual tickets and bespoke routing.

Use policy as code and evidence

Establish a small set of mandatory controls—identity, encryption, logging, network exposure, data residency and approved regions. Implement them through native policy engines and continuous compliance reporting, with a documented exception process.

Unify operational signals

Agree common requirements for logs, metrics, traces, audit events, retention and incident routing. Cloud-native tools can collect local telemetry, but service owners need a coherent cross-cloud view of customer impact and control health.

Offer paved roads

Reusable IaC modules, pipeline templates, secure base patterns and service catalogues reduce cognitive load. A platform team succeeds when product teams can move faster within visible guardrails.

Architecture outcome:

Governance becomes a reliable delivery capability instead of a manual approval stage.

Sequence the roadmap

  1. Baseline the current estate, ownership and risk.
  2. Define target account structures, identity and network patterns.
  3. Automate guardrails and operational telemetry.
  4. Launch a small set of high-demand paved roads.
  5. Migrate teams in waves and improve from measured feedback.
